# Fourier Optics for Inference

Could a Fourier-optical engine, which computes a 2-D Fourier transform (and with a mask, a convolution) in one pass of light, accelerate **disaggregated LLM inference**? This mini-series works it out from the physics up: what optics computes and what precision, conversions and static power cost; where transforms appear in inference workloads at all, with an **Amdahl analysis of prefill FLOP shares** from a published, tested script; and a discrete-event simulator with heterogeneous pools and an optical transform device in the prefill pool, with the break-even points and the KV hand-off options.

**Live index:** https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/LLM_Hub_Fourier_Optics_Inference/

## What is illustrative

The FLOP counts are exact for the stated conventions. The converter figures of merit (10 and 20 fJ per conversion step), the 1 pJ/FLOP digital energy, the GPU FFT efficiency ratios, the 2-megapixel mask device and its rewrite rates (1,031 Hz at 8-bit depth, about 20 kHz at 1-bit, tens of Hz for liquid crystal), the distilled state size, the hybrid ratio and the circulant block size are **illustrative**, and labelled where they are used. A block-circulant LLM at this scale is **speculative**: phase A of this work found no published one.

## How to read this series

Deck 01 is the physics and the bill: how optics computes a Fourier transform, and what precision, conversions and static power cost. Deck 02 asks where LLM inference has transforms at all, and answers with an Amdahl analysis computed by a published script. Deck 03 puts an optical transform device into a disaggregated-serving simulator's prefill pool. Every number in decks 01-02 comes from analysis/results.md, written by analysis/flop_share.py; every number in deck 03 from Disaggregated_Inference_Sim's examples/results.md. Illustrative coefficients are labelled where they are used.

> Optical-computing companies and this series. Several photonic-computing companies publicly describe systems for AI and for fully homomorphic encryption, and published optical computing for FHE centres on Fourier transforms (see FHESim 04). Everything here about applying a Fourier-optical engine to LLM inference is this series' own analysis and speculation, attributed to no company.

## Glossary: concepts and where they are explained

Every concept the decks rely on, with links to the slides that explain it in depth. The same glossary, with anchors each deck links to, is on the [live index](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/LLM_Hub_Fourier_Optics_Inference/#glossary).

| Concept | What it is | Where it is explained |
|---|---|---|
| The lens as a Fourier transformer | In coherent light, the field in a lens's back focal plane is the 2-D Fourier transform of the field in its front focal plane; a point at distance x from the axis holds spatial frequency x/(λf). The transform is computed by propagation, in one pass, for every pixel at once. | [FOptInf 01 · the lens as a Fourier transformer](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/FOptInf_01_Fourier_Optics_for_Engineers/#slide-02); [FOptInf 01 · the 4f system](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/FOptInf_01_Fourier_Optics_for_Engineers/#slide-03) |
| The convolution theorem | A convolution in one domain is a pointwise product in the other: F{h ∗ u} = F{h}·F{u}. A 4f system uses it optically (mask = filter spectrum); FFT convolution uses it digitally in O(n log n). | [FOptInf 01 · the 4f system and the convolution theorem](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/FOptInf_01_Fourier_Optics_for_Engineers/#slide-03); [FOptInf 01 · interactive FFT convolution](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/FOptInf_01_Fourier_Optics_for_Engineers/#slide-04); [FOptInf 02 · long convolutions](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/FOptInf_02_Transforms_in_Inference_Workloads/#slide-04) |
| Coherent and incoherent light | Coherent light (a laser) adds complex field amplitudes, so signed and complex values interfere; incoherent light adds non-negative intensities. Coherent systems compute Fourier transforms of fields; incoherent ones are limited to non-negative quantities unless values are offset or split. | [FOptInf 01 · coherent and incoherent light](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/FOptInf_01_Fourier_Optics_for_Engineers/#slide-05); [FOptInf 01 · detectors and the phase problem](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/FOptInf_01_Fourier_Optics_for_Engineers/#slide-07) |
| Spatial light modulator (SLM) | A pixelated device that writes values into a light field. Liquid-crystal SLMs modulate phase or amplitude and settle in tens of milliseconds; a 2-megapixel digital micromirror device switches amplitude at about 1 kHz with 8-bit depth (20 kHz binary). Pixel count and frame rate bound how fast inputs and Fourier-plane masks can change. | [FOptInf 01 · spatial light modulators](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/FOptInf_01_Fourier_Optics_for_Engineers/#slide-06); [FOptInf 02 · mask capacity](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/FOptInf_02_Transforms_in_Inference_Workloads/#slide-11) |
| Square-law detection and the phase problem | Photodetectors measure intensity |E|², losing the sign and phase of the field. Remedies: interfere with a reference beam (coherent detection), add a bias so the field stays positive, split positive and negative parts into two passes, or recover phase iteratively (phase retrieval). | [FOptInf 01 · detectors and the phase problem](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/FOptInf_01_Fourier_Optics_for_Engineers/#slide-07) |
| Mach–Zehnder interferometer (MZI) meshes | A triangular (Reck) or rectangular (Clements) grid of MZIs implements any N×N unitary, including the DFT, on a chip: N(N−1)/2 MZIs, each with a phase shifter that must be calibrated and held. | [FOptInf 01 · integrated-photonic alternatives](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/FOptInf_01_Fourier_Optics_for_Engineers/#slide-08) |
| Integrated optical Fourier transforms | On-chip alternatives to a free-space lens: diffractive or slab-lens structures, MZI meshes, and coupler and delay-line FFT circuits (used for all-optical OFDM). They are compact and fast but much smaller than a free-space 4f system. | [FOptInf 01 · integrated-photonic alternatives](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/FOptInf_01_Fourier_Optics_for_Engineers/#slide-08) |
| The noise rule for floating-point workloads | For float workloads the analogue chain must match the number format's own rounding error, not be exact. Relative RMS error = crest × 2^−ENOB × 2/√12; matching BF16 needs about 10.3 ENOB, INT8 8.0, FP8 E4M3 6.3. FHE's exact-rounding rule does not transfer to long convolutions. | [FOptInf 01 · precision: ENOB, noise and crosstalk](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/FOptInf_01_Fourier_Optics_for_Engineers/#slide-09); [FOptInf 02 · precision and energy per token](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/FOptInf_02_Transforms_in_Inference_Workloads/#slide-10) |
| Crest factor | The ratio of a signal's peak to its RMS value. An ADC's full scale must cover the peak, so a high crest factor wastes resolution on the rare large values: the noise rule's error scales with it. | [FOptInf 01 · precision: ENOB, noise and crosstalk](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/FOptInf_01_Fourier_Optics_for_Engineers/#slide-09) |
| Dynamic precision by repeated passes | Repeating an analogue operation k times and averaging cuts random noise by √k, buying ½ log2 k bits: one extra bit costs four times the passes. Digit planes, which make FHE exact, do not help a float workload. | [FOptInf 01 · buying precision: passes and planes](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/FOptInf_01_Fourier_Optics_for_Engineers/#slide-10); [FOptInf 03 · static power and energy per token](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/FOptInf_03_Optical_Prefill_Pools/#slide-08) |
| Conversion-bound optics and the break-even ENOB | Every value entering or leaving the optics costs a DAC or ADC sample, at Walden-FoM × 2^ENOB joules. With the series' illustrative FoMs a Hyena-style long convolution breaks even with a 1 pJ/FLOP digital FFT at about 12 ENOB. | [FOptInf 01 · conversion energy and static power](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/FOptInf_01_Fourier_Optics_for_Engineers/#slide-11); [FOptInf 02 · precision and energy per token](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/FOptInf_02_Transforms_in_Inference_Workloads/#slide-10); [FOptInf 03 · static power and energy per token](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/FOptInf_03_Optical_Prefill_Pools/#slide-08) |
| FNet | An encoder that replaces self-attention with an unparameterised 2-D Fourier transform over sequence and hidden dimensions, keeping the real part. It mixes every token with every other, including later ones, so it is not causal and cannot serve a decoder as it stands. | [FOptInf 02 · FNet: Fourier token mixing](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/FOptInf_02_Transforms_in_Inference_Workloads/#slide-03) |
| Long convolution by FFT | A convolution whose filter is as long as the sequence, computed by FFT, pointwise multiply and inverse FFT in O(L log L) instead of O(L²). It is the token mixer of S4's convolution mode, H3 and Hyena. | [FOptInf 02 · long convolutions](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/FOptInf_02_Transforms_in_Inference_Workloads/#slide-04); [FOptInf 01 · interactive FFT convolution](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/FOptInf_01_Fourier_Optics_for_Engineers/#slide-04) |
| Hyena | An attention-free operator: N+1 linear projections, a short depthwise convolution, then N rounds of a long implicit convolution (by FFT) and elementwise gating. Order 2 is the usual language-model setting. | [FOptInf 02 · long convolutions](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/FOptInf_02_Transforms_in_Inference_Workloads/#slide-04); [FOptInf 02 · the Amdahl analysis](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/FOptInf_02_Transforms_in_Inference_Workloads/#slide-06) |
| State-space models (S4, H3, Mamba) | Sequence layers built on a linear recurrence. S4's time-invariant SSM can run as a long convolution (for training and prefill) or as a recurrence (for generation); Mamba's selective SSM makes its parameters input-dependent, which rules out the convolution form. | [FOptInf 02 · long convolutions](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/FOptInf_02_Transforms_in_Inference_Workloads/#slide-04); [FOptInf 02 · what does not map](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/FOptInf_02_Transforms_in_Inference_Workloads/#slide-14) |
| Causal zero-padding | An FFT computes a circular convolution, which wraps late inputs onto early outputs. Padding input and filter to at least 2L before the FFT makes it the linear, causal convolution a decoder needs. | [FOptInf 02 · causality at prefill and at decode](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/FOptInf_02_Transforms_in_Inference_Workloads/#slide-05); [FOptInf 01 · interactive FFT convolution](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/FOptInf_01_Fourier_Optics_for_Engineers/#slide-04) |
| Relaxed (tiled) online convolution | Exact token-by-token convolution in which each completed block of 2^l inputs is convolved by FFT once and added to future outputs (Flash Inference): FFTs at decode, but small ones on the critical path. | [FOptInf 02 · causality at prefill and at decode](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/FOptInf_02_Transforms_in_Inference_Workloads/#slide-05) |
| Distilled recurrence (Laughing Hyena) | Fitting each long-convolution filter with a small state-space model after training, so decode runs as an O(1)-per-token recurrence with a constant-size state instead of a growing cache. | [FOptInf 02 · causality at prefill and at decode](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/FOptInf_02_Transforms_in_Inference_Workloads/#slide-05); [FOptInf 02 · what prefill hands to decode](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/FOptInf_02_Transforms_in_Inference_Workloads/#slide-12) |
| Block-circulant weights | A weight matrix made of circulant blocks, each defined by one vector, so multiplying by it is an FFT, a pointwise product and an inverse FFT (CirCNN). It turns matmuls into transforms; at LLM scale it is speculative. | [FOptInf 02 · structured weights](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/FOptInf_02_Transforms_in_Inference_Workloads/#slide-08) |
| Monarch and butterfly matrices | Structured matrices built from products of block-diagonal and permutation factors, a family that includes the FFT. Monarch Mixer uses them along both sequence and hidden dimensions, with a causal variant. | [FOptInf 02 · structured weights](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/FOptInf_02_Transforms_in_Inference_Workloads/#slide-08) |
| Hybrid architectures | Models that interleave a few attention layers with cheaper mixers (Hyena, Mamba). They keep attention's recall where it matters; the transform share falls with the attention fraction. | [FOptInf 02 · the Amdahl analysis](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/FOptInf_02_Transforms_in_Inference_Workloads/#slide-06) |
| Optical share and the Amdahl bound | The fraction f of prefill FLOPs a transform engine could take over (FFTs plus Fourier-plane multiplies). If it became free, prefill would speed up by at most 1/(1−f); for a Hyena-2 model of Llama-3-8B's shape f is 0.20% at 2,048 tokens. | [FOptInf 02 · the Amdahl analysis](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/FOptInf_02_Transforms_in_Inference_Workloads/#slide-06); [FOptInf 02 · interactive Amdahl explorer](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/FOptInf_02_Transforms_in_Inference_Workloads/#slide-07) |
| Time-weighted share | FFTs run below a GPU's matmul rate, so their share of time exceeds their share of FLOPs. Weighting transform FLOPs by a relative efficiency r (an assumption) gives the share an engine could actually remove. | [FOptInf 02 · the Amdahl analysis](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/FOptInf_02_Transforms_in_Inference_Workloads/#slide-06); [FOptInf 02 · interactive Amdahl explorer](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/FOptInf_02_Transforms_in_Inference_Workloads/#slide-07) |
| Fourier-plane mask capacity | A 4f pass multiplies by one mask, so every filter spectrum must be on the mask while its inputs pass. Mask values needed per forward pass divided by what the SLM holds gives rewrites per pass, each costing a frame. | [FOptInf 02 · mask capacity](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/FOptInf_02_Transforms_in_Inference_Workloads/#slide-11); [FOptInf 03 · mask rewrites in the simulator](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/FOptInf_03_Optical_Prefill_Pools/#slide-07) |
| Transform-domain KV compression | Compressing the KV cache or hidden states by keeping low-frequency DCT/Fourier coefficients along the sequence (FreqKV, FourierAttention, Fourier Transformer). It saves memory; its transforms are small. | [FOptInf 02 · transform-domain compression](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/FOptInf_02_Transforms_in_Inference_Workloads/#slide-09) |
| Prefill-to-decode hand-off | What a disaggregated server moves from the prefill pool to the decode pool: the KV cache for attention, the cached projection inputs for direct long-convolution decode (four times larger here), or a constant recurrent state after distillation. | [FOptInf 02 · what prefill hands to decode](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/FOptInf_02_Transforms_in_Inference_Workloads/#slide-12); [FOptInf 03 · the KV hand-off in the simulator](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/FOptInf_03_Optical_Prefill_Pools/#slide-11) |
| Transform engine against optical MAC | An optical MAC accelerates every matrix multiply (the simulator's hypothetical optical part); a Fourier transform engine accelerates only FFT and convolution work and leaves matmuls to a digital part. | [FOptInf 02 · a standard decoder has no FFT](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/FOptInf_02_Transforms_in_Inference_Workloads/#slide-02); [FOptInf 02 · why disaggregation fits](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/FOptInf_02_Transforms_in_Inference_Workloads/#slide-13); [FOptInf 03 · the transform engine model](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/FOptInf_03_Optical_Prefill_Pools/#slide-03) |
| Photonic interconnect | Optical links (co-packaged optics, optical I/O chiplets) that move bytes, such as the KV cache, between chips. They are photonics but not Fourier optics: they compute nothing. | [FOptInf 02 · why disaggregation fits](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/FOptInf_02_Transforms_in_Inference_Workloads/#slide-13); [FOptInf 03 · the KV hand-off in the simulator](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/FOptInf_03_Optical_Prefill_Pools/#slide-11) |
| Heterogeneous pools | Disaggregated serving with different hardware per phase: compute-heavy parts for prefill, bandwidth-rich parts for decode (Splitwise's proposal). An optical transform engine would join the prefill pool. | [FOptInf 03 · heterogeneous pools in the simulator](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/FOptInf_03_Optical_Prefill_Pools/#slide-04); [FOptInf 02 · why disaggregation fits](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/FOptInf_02_Transforms_in_Inference_Workloads/#slide-13); [LLM Inference Simulators 05 · limitations and extensions](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/InfSim_05_Disaggregated_Inference/#slide-10) |
| GPU FFT efficiency | The fraction of its matmul rate a GPU reaches on FFT and Fourier-domain work. The simulator's default (1) is optimistic for GPUs; at 1/16 the GPU's own prefill of a transform-heavy model slows enough for an optical engine to have something to win. It moves the circulant verdict more than any optical parameter. | [FOptInf 03 · where the time goes](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/FOptInf_03_Optical_Prefill_Pools/#slide-07); [FOptInf 03 · the break-even point](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/FOptInf_03_Optical_Prefill_Pools/#slide-09) |
| Break-even point | The value of a parameter (static power, ENOB, mask rate, GPU FFT efficiency) at which an optical prefill pool's J/token or TTFT p99 equals the all-GPU baseline's, found by bisection on full simulator runs. | [FOptInf 03 · the break-even point](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/FOptInf_03_Optical_Prefill_Pools/#slide-09) |
| Compute in transit | Operations applied in a link's data path, between transmitter and receiver, on data that must move anyway. At about 1–2 operations per byte at line rate it suits requantising or frequency-compressing the KV hand-off, not matmuls; it helps latency only where the link is the bottleneck. Speculative here. | [FOptInf 03 · compute in the transport](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/FOptInf_03_Optical_Prefill_Pools/#slide-12); [FOptInf 03 · in transit or at the GPU](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/FOptInf_03_Optical_Prefill_Pools/#slide-13) |
